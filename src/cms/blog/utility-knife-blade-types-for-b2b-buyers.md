Blade type is one of the most important decisions when sourcing utility knives. It affects cutting performance, safety, replacement demand, packaging, and repeat sales. For importers, hardware distributors, warehouse suppliers, and private-label tool brands, the best utility knife is not always the most expensive model. It is the model that fits the target user and sales channel.

wooktools supplies WOOKTOOL utility knives, box cutters, snap-off knives, safety cutters, and replacement blade packs for global B2B buyers. This guide explains common blade directions and what buyers should confirm before ordering.

## 9mm snap-off blades

9mm snap-off blades are commonly used for light cutting, stationery, packaging, office supply, and small DIY tasks. They are usually paired with compact cutter handles and simple retail packaging.

This direction can be suitable when buyers need a lightweight product, lower unit cost, or compact shelf display. However, it may not be the best option for heavy carton work or industrial cutting.

For 9mm cutter programs, buyers should confirm blade thickness, sliding track stability, lock function, spare blade quantity, and packaging.

## 18mm snap-off blades

18mm snap-off blades are often used for stronger daily cutting tasks. They are common in hardware stores, warehouse supply, renovation work, and general tool programs.

Compared with 9mm blades, 18mm blades can provide a stronger cutting feel. The handle structure, automatic lock, blade storage, and replacement process should be checked carefully during sampling.

For B2B sourcing, buyers should ask whether the model supports replacement blades, whether the handle can hold spare blades, and whether the blade lock feels stable during practical cutting.

## Trapezoid utility blades

Trapezoid blades are common in heavy-duty utility knives and box cutters. They are often used for carton opening, warehouse cutting, construction tasks, and retail tool programs.

This blade direction can create a stronger product impression, especially when paired with alloy handles, folding handles, quick-change mechanisms, or blade storage. It is also useful for replacement blade pack programs because end users may buy blade refills repeatedly.

Buyers should confirm blade fit, quick-change structure, folding lock, blade storage, and safety mechanism before production.

## SK5 and selected blade materials

Blade material affects sharpness, durability, and positioning. SK5 is a common option for utility knife blades and replacement blade packs. Depending on product grade and target price, buyers may also discuss other selected steel directions.

Material choice should be matched with the product program. A retail DIY cutter, warehouse cutter, and replacement blade pack may not need the same blade direction. Buyers should review target price, end user expectation, cutting scenario, and packaging format together.

## Replacement blade packs

Replacement blade packs can support repeat sales for distributors and retailers. Buyers should confirm blade size, blade material, blade color, oiling or rust-prevention needs, pack quantity, barcode label, and warning information.

Common B2B questions include:

- How many blades should be packed together?
- Will the blades be sold separately or included with the knife?
- Does the buyer need private-label packaging?
- Should the blade pack match an existing utility knife model?

## Quality checks before shipment

Utility knife quality should not be judged only by appearance. Buyers should inspect blade sharpness, blade replacement fit, lock function, handle assembly, sliding movement, folding action, safety guard, packaging, barcode, quantity, and carton condition.

For workplace or warehouse programs, blade exposure and lock stability are especially important. For retail programs, blister card quality, warning text, barcode placement, and carton marks should also be reviewed.
