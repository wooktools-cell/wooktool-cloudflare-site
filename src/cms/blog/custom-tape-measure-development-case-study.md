A customer came to WOOKTOOL with a concept for a gift tape measure. The finished product needed its own shape, a custom printed scale and a box. The case takes you from the initial concept through mold design, toolmaking, sample measurement and inspection. It shows what to discuss with a factory when the shape of the product is part of the brief and there is no catalog model to point to.

## A reference image is enough to start the discussion

You can start a custom tape measure development project with a sketch, photo or sample. Mark the parts that matter to you. If your reference is a photo, circle the shape you want to keep and note which details the factory can change. If the tape needs specific dimensions, units or markings, list them alongside the image so they carry through to the sample brief.

In the gift tape measure project, the customer’s concept guided the mold design. If you're deciding between an existing model and a new shape, ask the factory to review both routes. A new outer shape or structural change may call for new tooling. A different logo, printed scale or package may be possible on an existing model, depending on the configuration. [Explore the options for customizing an existing tape measure.](/custom-tape-measure/)

## Ask what the mold has to do before approving tooling

The project records show mold design and toolmaking as separate steps. Once you have a mold drawing to review, check it against the part you actually want. If the outline needs to match a gift concept or fit into a display, put those dimensions in the brief. Ask the factory what can be agreed from the drawing and what needs to be checked on a sample.

Before authorizing tooling, ask for the mold, sample rounds and first production run to be priced separately. Discuss who owns and stores the mold, what happens if you request a change, and what quantity applies to production. The answers belong in the quotation or project agreement so both sides can refer back to them. [See the full gift tape measure development case.](/custom-tool-projects/#development)

## Check the sample, the scale and the box as separate items

A photograph can show the shape and finish of a sample. If a dimension matters, ask the factory to measure it against the agreed drawing. The gift tape measure case includes a sample measurement step as well as a custom printed scale and packaging. Each needs its own review.

| What to check | What to agree with the factory |
| --- | --- |
| Sample dimensions | Which dimensions to measure, how to record them, and what tolerances apply |
| Printed scale | Units, markings and layout, plus any accuracy or test requirements for the intended use |
| Finished tool and box | Whether the tool fits the box, how the artwork looks on the finished pack, and what labels the sales channel requires |
| Inspection | Which dimensions, functions and packaging checks belong in the inspection brief |
