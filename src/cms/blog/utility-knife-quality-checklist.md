## Short answer

Utility knife quality depends on blade sharpness, blade stability, locking function, handle strength, sliding or folding mechanism, surface finish, and packaging accuracy. For B2B buyers, the inspection checklist should match the sales channel: retail stores, industrial supply, warehouse use, construction use, or private-label packaging.

## Quality checklist

| Checkpoint | What to confirm | Buyer risk if ignored |
|---|---|---|
| Blade sharpness | Cutting performance and blade edge condition | Weak cutting experience and complaints |
| Lock function | Slider, folding lock, or safety lock stability | Safety risk and product returns |
| Handle material | ABS, zinc alloy, rubber grip, or mixed material | Durability and perceived value issues |
| Blade movement | Smooth extension, retraction, or folding | Poor user experience |
| Spare blades | Quantity, size, packing method | Missing accessories or listing mismatch |
| Packaging | Blister card, color box, barcode, warning text | Retail listing delays or repacking |

## RFQ notes

Buyers should send reference photos and explain whether the knife is for light use, warehouse cutting, construction work, hardware retail, or promotional programs. The best model may differ by sales channel.

## FAQ

### What is the most common utility knife quality problem?

Common issues include weak lock function, loose blade movement, inconsistent surface finish, packaging errors, and blade pack mismatch.

### Should buyers inspect packaging as part of utility knife QC?

Yes. Retail packaging, warning labels, barcode position, carton marks, and spare blade information should be checked before shipment.
