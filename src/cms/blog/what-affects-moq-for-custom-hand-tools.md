You ask for the minimum order quantity (MOQ) for a folding utility knife with your logo. The factory gives you a number. Then you send the artwork for a printed retail box, and the minimum changes. The knife is the same. The order the factory is pricing has changed.

When you buy custom hand tools in bulk, ask which part of your specification sets the minimum. It could be the tool, a custom component or color, the printed packaging, or the setup needed for a new design. Once you know what sets the floor, you can decide what to keep in the first order and what to revisit after launch.

## Ask what the MOQ applies to

A minimum order quantity is the smallest production order a factory will accept for a specified configuration. Ask whether the minimum is **per model, per color, per packaging design, or across the whole order**. A total that works for one color may be quoted differently if you split it across several. If a printed box has its own minimum, ask whether you are paying for extra boxes and what happens to them after the first run.

| What you want to buy | What can affect the minimum | What to ask the factory |
|---|---|---|
| A stock model with your logo | Logo application, placement and setup | Can you quote this model with our logo and your standard packaging? |
| A specified color or component | A separate color run or a component ordered for your configuration | What is the minimum per color or component? Which options are already available for this model? |
| A tool in a custom retail box | Packaging print quantity and the number of artwork versions | What is the minimum for each box version? Can you quote standard and printed packaging separately? |
| A new design with custom tooling | Tool design, mold making, sample revisions and the first production run | Can you quote tooling, samples and first-run production separately? What are the mold ownership and storage terms? |

For a trial order, ask for two quotes based on the **same tool, quantity and delivery terms**. One should use the branding and packaging you need for launch. The other can include options you would consider for a repeat order. Ask what each quote includes for sampling, printing and inspection. This gives you a specific trade-off to discuss with your team before you commit.

## Existing tooling: a folding knife with a new brand identity

In one WOOKTOOL project, the buyer supplied a logo and started with an existing folding utility knife. The project shows the logo placement, print artwork, a design mockup, the finished product and color samples. Seeing the logo on the actual handle and reviewing the retail artwork gave the buyer two separate items to check.

**What to take into your own RFQ:** When sourcing private label hand tools, send the model link or a reference photo, mark where the logo should go, and say which packaging you need for launch. If several colors are acceptable, identify your first choice and ask which colors are available at your target quantity. Include any required blade or component in the same request so the factory can quote the configuration you intend to buy. ## New tooling: a gift tape measure developed from a concept

The second buyer began with a concept for a gift tape measure. The project records show mold design and making, measurement of a sample, a custom printed scale and box, the finished product, and an inspection stage. The shape, scale and packaging each gave the buyer something different to review.

**What to take into your own RFQ:** If the shape or function defines the product, share a sketch, sample or annotated reference photo and mark the features that matter. For a tape measure, specify the intended scale and which dimensions need to be checked. Ask what can be confirmed from the drawing, what needs a physical sample, and which decisions are needed before making or modifying the mold. ## How to reduce the back-and-forth with a factory

Short messages work well for quick questions. The problem starts when the quote is in one email, the latest logo is in a chat thread, and the packaging change was agreed on a call. By the time the sample arrives, the buyer and factory may be working from different versions.

Send a single request for quotation (RFQ) with a revision date. Keep the brief short enough that the factory can reply against each item:

- **Product and quantity.** Include the model link or an annotated reference, the quantity per variant, and an estimated repeat quantity if you have one.
- **What must match the brief.** List the required specifications, components, colors, logo placement and packaging. Mark the details where you can accept alternatives.
- **Sales market and channel.** Give the destination country and say whether the product is for retail shelves, distribution or online sales. Include any known label languages, barcode and carton requirements.
- **What needs approval.** Identify the artwork, color reference, sample measurements and packaging details you want to check. After a change, confirm which revision the quotation and sample refer to.

Try replacing “Can you do a lower MOQ?” with “Which part of this configuration sets the minimum? If we keep the tool and logo the same, what changes if we switch to your standard box?” The factory can answer against a specific order, and you can compare that answer with the version already on your desk.
